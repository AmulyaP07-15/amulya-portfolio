## The Problem Worth Solving

When I started this project, I had one question: *which feature extraction strategy actually works best for medical images?* Not in theory — in practice, at scale, with real clinical data distributions.

Medical images are unlike natural images. A chest X-ray encodes subtle density variations that carry enormous diagnostic weight. A histopathology slide at 40x magnification contains millions of cells where a 2% spatial difference can mean the difference between benign and malignant.

Generic vision models trained on ImageNet don't understand this. So we need to benchmark.

## What We Built

We evaluated three major families of feature extractors on a standardized medical image dataset:

- **CNN-based backbones** (ResNet-50, DenseNet-121) pretrained on ImageNet and CheXpert
- **Vision Transformers (ViT)** — both vanilla and medical-domain fine-tuned
- **BioBERT-derived multimodal models** — combining radiology reports with image patches

The goal: find which strategy produces the most semantically meaningful embeddings for retrieval and classification.

## Key Metrics

We used three evaluation axes:

1. **Precision@5** — are the 5 nearest neighbors in embedding space clinically similar?
2. **Davies-Bouldin Index** — how well-separated are clusters in the latent space?
3. **Linear probe accuracy** — how much information is encoded linearly?

## Results That Surprised Me

The biggest surprise: **domain-adapted ViTs significantly outperformed ImageNet-pretrained CNNs**, even with far fewer parameters. The ViT-B/16 fine-tuned on CheXpert embeddings showed a **58.7% improvement in Precision@5** over the ResNet-50 ImageNet baseline.

Even more striking: the **Davies-Bouldin Index dropped by 78.7%** — meaning the embedding clusters were dramatically tighter and better separated. This matters for retrieval systems where you want semantically similar cases grouped together.

# Simplified evaluation loop
for model_name, extractor in extractors.items():

## What I'd Do Differently

1. **Self-supervised pretraining on in-domain data** — DINO or MAE on a private medical corpus would likely push results further
2. **Contrastive learning with clinical metadata** — pairing images with structured EHR data as supervision signal
3. **Hierarchical retrieval** — first retrieve by modality/body part, then by pathology

## Takeaways

Building this system taught me that benchmarking is not just about numbers — it's about understanding *why* certain representations generalize. Domain shift is real, and for high-stakes domains like medicine, generic embeddings aren't good enough.

The intersection of self-supervised learning and medical AI is where I want to push next.
